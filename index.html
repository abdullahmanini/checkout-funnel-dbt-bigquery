Recommendation

Ship guest checkout with a 10% holdout.

  1. Conversion rose 3.53 points, from 45.25% to 48.78%, with p < 0.001. The estimate lands within 0.25 points of the effect built into the simulation.
  2. Revenue per visitor rose 5.5% but the interval still crosses zero (p = 0.085). Eight weeks could detect about 9%, so keep a holdout and read it again next quarter.
  3. Add an account prompt after a guest order. Guest buyers can't be linked to a later order, which drags the measured repeat rate down.

02 · Simulated A/B test

Guest checkout lifts conversion; revenue needs a longer read.

Visitors from August 3 to September 27, 2026 were split 50/50 with a salted FARM_FINGERPRINT hash: 8,794 control and 8,789 treatment (sample ratio check p = 0.97). In treatment, 12% of anonymous carts became guest orders at list price minus a 10% welcome code, which produced 278 orders. Everything else is the store's own data, and the true effect is known because the mart computes the counterfactual for both arms.

Why Welch decides revenue

Mann-Whitney U on revenue per visitor gives p < 0.001 and Welch's t-test gives 0.085. Most visitors spend $0, so Mann-Whitney mostly detects the conversion change. Welch answers whether dollars per visitor moved.

By traffic source

The lift is positive in all five sources. After Holm correction only AdWords and Email clear 0.05, and they are the two largest groups, so this looks like sample size rather than a reason to target by channel.

Custom tests

  • A purchase never comes before its cart add
  • Every purchase session has an order line
  • Guest orders never appear in control
  • Session-to-order matches over 10 minutes apart (warn)

Things the data needed

thelook has no session id on orders, so each purchase session is matched to the same user's order line for a product viewed in that session, nearest in time. The public table also holds events dated in the future (1,377 on November 16, 2026), so staging cuts at a fixed as_of date.

05 · Limits

What is real and what is simulated.

thelook_ecommerce is a synthetic store Google publishes for practice, so its patterns are cleaner than real shoppers: anonymous sessions never buy and segments hardly differ. The funnel, GMV and brand numbers are what that dataset contains.

The guest orders in the test are invented by a fixed rule, and the 12% recovery rate and 10% discount are assumptions. The test shows the method recovering a known answer. It says nothing about how a real guest checkout would perform.
